# Introduction to PIPEDA

The **Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA)** is Canada's federal private-sector privacy law. It governs how organizations collect, use, disclose, retain, and protect personal information during commercial activities.

Although privacy law reform continues to evolve in Canada, PIPEDA remains the cornerstone of private-sector privacy compliance and reflects internationally recognized privacy principles.

---

# Canadian Privacy Landscape

Canada's privacy framework is often described as a **mosaic of privacy laws** due to the division of authority between federal and provincial governments.

Privacy regulation applies differently depending on:

* The type of organization
* The province of operation
* Whether the organization operates in a federally regulated industry
* The type of personal information involved

## Public Sector Privacy Laws

Government institutions are regulated by public-sector privacy legislation.

### Federal Government

Governed by the Privacy Act, which applies to:

* Federal departments
* Agencies
* Crown corporations

### Provincial Governments

Each province maintains its own public-sector privacy legislation governing:

* Provincial ministries
* Municipalities
* Universities
* Schools
* Hospitals

---

## Private Sector Privacy Laws

Private organizations may be regulated by:

### Federal Law

PIPEDA applies to:

* Commercial activities
* Federally regulated businesses

### Provincial Privacy Laws

Certain provinces have enacted legislation deemed substantially similar to PIPEDA:

* Quebec
* Alberta
* British Columbia

In these provinces, provincial legislation generally replaces PIPEDA for intra-provincial private-sector activities.

---

# Constitutional Foundations of Privacy Law in Canada

Canada's privacy framework is heavily influenced by its constitutional division of powers.

## Provincial Authority

Provinces have jurisdiction over:

* Property rights
* Civil rights
* Most privacy matters

## Federal Authority

The federal government has jurisdiction over:

* General trade and commerce
* Federal works, undertakings, and businesses (FWUBs)

Examples include:

* Banks
* Airlines
* Telecommunications companies
* Interprovincial transportation providers

This division creates overlapping privacy responsibilities between federal and provincial governments.

---

# Origins of PIPEDA

PIPEDA was largely built upon the **Canadian Standards Association (CSA) Model Code for the Protection of Personal Information**.

The CSA Model Code:

* Was developed through extensive consultations
* Became a national privacy standard in 1996
* Drew inspiration from OECD privacy guidelines
* Established ten privacy principles that remain the foundation of PIPEDA today

PIPEDA incorporated these principles directly into federal law.

---

# Structure of PIPEDA

PIPEDA consists of two primary components:

## Part 1 – Personal Information Protection

Regulates:

* Collection
* Use
* Disclosure
* Retention
* Protection of personal information

## Part 2 – Electronic Documents

Provides legal recognition of electronic documents and electronic transactions.

# Key Concepts Under PIPEDA

## Commercial Activity

PIPEDA applies primarily to the collection, use, and disclosure of personal information in the course of commercial activities.

Examples include:

* Selling products
* Delivering services
* Marketing activities
* Customer management

---

## Federal Works, Undertakings, and Businesses (FWUBs)

PIPEDA also regulates employee personal information within federally regulated organizations such as:

* Banks
* Airlines
* Telecommunications providers
* Railways

---

## Personal Information

Personal information means:

> Information about an identifiable individual.

Examples include:

* Names
* Addresses
* Email addresses
* Social Insurance Numbers
* Photographs
* Health records
* Financial information
* Biometric data

The definition is intentionally broad.

If an individual can reasonably be identified from the information, it is likely personal information.

---

## Reasonableness Requirement

PIPEDA contains a foundational rule:

Organizations may only collect, use, or disclose personal information for purposes that a reasonable person would consider appropriate in the circumstances.

Even with consent, unreasonable purposes are prohibited.

# The 10 Privacy Principles

---

## Principle 1: Accountability

Organizations are responsible for personal information under their control and must designate an individual accountable for privacy compliance.

### Requirements

Organizations must:

* Appoint a Privacy Officer
* Develop privacy governance programs
* Monitor compliance
* Handle complaints
* Serve as liaison with regulators

### Transfer vs Disclosure

#### Transfer

Information is shared with a service provider acting on behalf of the organization.

* No new purpose
* No additional consent generally required

#### Disclosure

Information is shared with another organization for its own purposes.

* New purpose
* Consent typically required

Organizations remain accountable for information processed by third-party vendors.

---

## Principle 2: Identifying Purposes

Organizations must identify and communicate the purposes for collecting personal information before or at the time of collection.

### Requirements

Organizations must:

1. Define collection purposes
2. Document purposes internally
3. Explain purposes to individuals

Purpose identification forms the foundation for consent and all subsequent privacy obligations.

---

## Principle 3: Consent

Knowledge and consent are required for the collection, use, and disclosure of personal information unless a statutory exception applies.

### Consent Must Be

* Meaningful
* Informed
* Understandable
* Appropriate to the sensitivity of the information

### Types of Consent

#### Implied Consent

Appropriate where:

* Information is less sensitive
* Purpose is obvious

#### Express Consent

Recommended for:

* Health information
* Financial information
* Biometric information
* Other sensitive data

### Withdrawal of Consent

Individuals may withdraw consent at any time, subject to legal and operational limitations.

Organizations must explain the consequences of withdrawal.

---

## Principle 4: Limiting Collection

Organizations may collect only the information necessary to achieve identified purposes.

### Requirements

Collection must be:

* Necessary
* Proportionate
* Relevant
* Lawful

Organizations should follow the principle of data minimization.

---

## Principle 5: Limiting Use, Disclosure, and Retention

Organizations may use, disclose, and retain personal information only for identified purposes.

### Requirements

Organizations must:

* Restrict use to stated purposes
* Obtain new consent for new purposes
* Retain information only as long as necessary
* Securely dispose of information when no longer needed

### Retention Management

Organizations should maintain:

* Retention schedules
* Destruction procedures
* Data lifecycle management practices

### Anonymization

Properly anonymized information falls outside the scope of PIPEDA.

---

## Principle 6: Accuracy

Personal information must be accurate, complete, and current to the extent necessary for its intended use.

### Requirements

Organizations should:

* Verify information when making decisions
* Correct inaccuracies
* Allow individuals to challenge incorrect information

Accuracy is particularly important when information influences decisions affecting individuals.

---

## Principle 7: Safeguards

Organizations must protect personal information using safeguards appropriate to the sensitivity of the information.

### Security Objectives

Protect against:

* Loss
* Theft
* Unauthorized access
* Unauthorized disclosure
* Unauthorized modification
* Unauthorized use

### Types of Safeguards

#### Physical

* Locked offices
* Secure storage

#### Administrative

* Policies
* Employee training
* Access controls

#### Technical

* Encryption
* Multi-factor authentication
* Monitoring systems

PIPEDA follows a risk-based and technology-neutral approach.

---

## Principle 8: Openness

Organizations must make information about their privacy practices readily available.

### Privacy Notices Should Include

* Categories of information collected
* Purposes of collection
* Use and disclosure practices
* Contact information for the Privacy Officer
* Instructions for exercising privacy rights

### Best Practice

Organizations should also disclose:

* Cross-border transfers
* International storage locations
* Third-party service providers

---

## Principle 9: Individual Access

Individuals have the right to access personal information held about them.

### Individuals May

* Request access
* Verify accuracy
* Request corrections
* Learn how information has been used
* Learn who has received disclosures

### Organizational Obligations

Organizations generally must respond within 30 days.

### Exceptions

Access may be limited when information contains:

* Third-party information
* Privileged material
* Confidential business information

---

## Principle 10: Challenging Compliance

Individuals must be able to challenge an organization's compliance with privacy obligations.

### Requirements

Organizations must:

* Maintain complaint procedures
* Investigate concerns
* Respond appropriately
* Inform individuals of escalation options

An effective complaint process often prevents regulatory escalation.

---

# PIPEDA Enforcement Framework

## Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (OPC)

PIPEDA is overseen by the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.

The Commissioner operates primarily under an ombudsman model.

### Powers Include

* Investigating complaints
* Compelling evidence
* Summoning witnesses
* Examining records
* Entering business premises (excluding dwellings)

---

## Investigation Outcomes

### Not Well-Founded

No privacy violation identified.

### Well-Founded and Resolved

Issue identified and corrected.

### Well-Founded and Conditionally Resolved

Corrective actions remain ongoing.

### Well-Founded and Unresolved

Matter may proceed to Federal Court.

---

## Federal Court Remedies

The Federal Court may:

* Order compliance
* Require corrective actions
* Require public notices
* Award damages

Court proceedings are conducted on a de novo basis, meaning the court reviews the matter from the beginning.

# Data Breach Reporting and Notification

## Breach of Security Safeguards

A breach includes:

* Unauthorized access
* Unauthorized disclosure
* Loss of personal information
* Failure to implement adequate safeguards

---

## Real Risk of Significant Harm (RROSH)

Organizations must assess whether a breach creates a Real Risk of Significant Harm.

### Risk Assessment Factors

#### Sensitivity

Examples:

* Health information
* Financial information
* Identity information

#### Probability of Misuse

Examples:

* Malicious cyberattack
* Insider theft
* Credential compromise

---

## Significant Harm

Potential harms include:

* Identity theft
* Financial loss
* Reputational damage
* Employment impacts
* Relationship harm
* Emotional distress
* Physical harm

---

## Breach Reporting Requirements

Organizations must report qualifying breaches to the Privacy Commissioner.

Reports should include:

* Description of the breach
* Information involved
* Number of affected individuals
* Mitigation measures
* Contact information

---

## Individual Notification Requirements

Affected individuals must receive notice containing:

* Description of the breach
* Risks involved
* Mitigation steps taken
* Recommended actions

Examples:

* Password changes
* Credit monitoring
* Fraud alerts

---

## Mandatory Breach Recordkeeping

Organizations must document every breach of security safeguards.

This applies regardless of whether notification is required.

### Records Should Include

* Incident details
* Information involved
* Risk assessment
* Decision rationale
* Corrective actions

### Retention Requirement

Breach records must be retained for a minimum of two years.

Failure to maintain required records constitutes an offence under PIPEDA.

# Key Takeaways for Privacy Professionals

1. Accountability cannot be outsourced.
2. Clearly identified purposes drive compliance.
3. Meaningful consent remains the primary legal basis for processing.
4. Collect only the information necessary for legitimate purposes.
5. Limit use, disclosure, and retention.
6. Maintain accurate information when making decisions.
7. Implement risk-based security safeguards.
8. Be transparent through privacy notices and communications.
9. Respect access and correction rights.
10. Establish complaint handling and breach response procedures.

---

# Conclusion

PIPEDA establishes a principles-based privacy framework that balances individual privacy rights with legitimate business needs. Its ten privacy principles create a comprehensive governance model built around accountability, transparency, consent, data minimization, security, and individual rights.

For privacy professionals, compliance requires more than simply publishing a privacy policy. Organizations must embed privacy into governance structures, operational processes, vendor management, security programs, retention practices, and incident response procedures.

Ultimately, successful PIPEDA compliance depends on demonstrating accountability throughout the entire lifecycle of personal information.
